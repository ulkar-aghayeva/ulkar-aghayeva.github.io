  <div><p>Byrd and Gibbons represent the English Virginalist School which refers to a group of composers of the Elizabethan and Jacobean periods who wrote keyboard music. Keyboards of the time included virginals, muselars, clavichords, harpsichords and (chamber) organs. All of them, except for organs, have a similar mechanism: wire strings of various lengths plucked by plectra upon pressing of the keys. Virginals are box-shaped and have a smaller keyboard than harpsichords proper. </p></div>

  <div><p>Unlike <em>fortepiano</em> which is a percussion instrument that was invented later, around 1700, the harpsichord family of keyboards are plucked string instruments similar to lute and guitar [1]. Since plucking always happens with the same strength, these instruments have a constant dynamic range, so you can’t play them softer or louder than the baseline in principle. But keyboardists can still mimic the dynamic capabilities of a piano through manipulations of time — with delays to convey softness and speeding up to imitate crescendo. </p><p>The art of playing harpsichord is to a large extent about the gap between the written and performed score. As the pianist Jeremy Denk writes in his autobiography <em>Every Good Boy Does Fine</em>:</p><blockquote><p> …there is an important distinction between rhythms written on the page and the rhythms that you actually play.&nbsp; &lt;…&gt; A computer program can play back for you what’s mathematically written—it sounds horrible. The rhythms on the page of music, interpreted literally, are lifeless, or worse than lifeless, like a zombie. If you play metronomically 'right,' it is musically wrong. &lt;…&gt; Once a real person plays, though, a million small “errors” creep in. Certain notes come a bit sooner, others a bit later—microscopic deviations, handfuls of nanoseconds—and suddenly we have what is called, for lack of a better word, phrasing.</p><p>The performed rhythm is like a dance around the written rhythm, or a shading around it. This gap between written and performed is not a failing; it is the classical musician’s only hope of success. &lt;…&gt;&nbsp; the performer has two tasks: one is to do what’s written in the score—incredibly important; and the other, even more important, is to find everything that’s not.</p></blockquote><p>What’s not found in the score is especially important in harpsichord music where, absent dynamic capabilities, one can only rely on temporal devices to bring a piece to life.  </p><p>Another peculiarity of the harpsichord family of keyboards is the relatively quick decay of sound from the plucked strings, compared to piano which can sustain sound though hammering of the strings and pedaling. This might be why Renaissance and even moreso, Baroque keyboard music is so rich in ornament: thrills, mordents and the like are a way of extending the duration of sound that would otherwise decay too quickly. This is an example of aesthetic choices influenced by technical limitations.</p><p>Works of EVS composers have reached us in several collections: Fitzwilliam Virginal Book, My Ladye Nevells Booke, The Mulliner Book and others. I’ll go over some of their most notable works below. These include not only keyboard music but also choral works and consort music. </p><p>Note: all links are from Apple Music (with a few exceptions of live recordings).</p><p><strong>William Byrd</strong> (c. 1540–1623) is a composer to whom I owe one of the most transformative experiences of my life. In 2019 I attended a <a href="https://www.groupmuse.com/" rel="noopener">Groupmuse</a> where a cello quartet played a selection of pieces ranging from Renaissance to Romanticism. The reason I decided to go to this concert was Byrd’s choral work <em>Ave Verum Corpus</em> whose cello quartet arrangement was in the program. As I was listening to it, I had a distinct feeling of something extraordinary happening, of the ground shifting under my feet, of an uncontainable awe welling up in my heart. I could not remain passive in response to this transfiguration, I had to do something about it. After some days of restlessness, I realized that I must start writing music myself. Byrd — and that cello quartet — are the reason I became a composer. </p><p>There’s <a href="https://youtu.be/BsRdkdjw3fQ?si=Ldv9KjbV4S2Hm5OR&amp;t=13" rel="noopener">a recording</a> of this fateful performance on youtube, so you can perhaps relive it with me (I recommend listening to the <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rbIhmrOOENI" rel="noopener">original choral</a> first). </p><p>Many of Byrd’s choral works are the fruit of his Catholic faith in a largely Protestant Elizabethan England. To her credit, Elizabeth I proclaimed, "I have no desire to make windows into men's souls”, and Byrd could practice his faith without major repercussions. But there’s an undercurrent of themes of persecution and martyrdom in his <em>Cantiones Sacrae</em>, possibly an expression of laments of the Catholic community. Byrd also wrote Anglican church service music which continued to be performed and built on after his death, unlike the Latin motets. </p><p>Choral works:</p><p><a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/ave-verum-corpus/263774075?i=263776467" rel="noopener">Ave Verum Corpus</a> | <a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/infelix-ego/263774075?i=263776732" rel="noopener">Infelix Ego</a> | <a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/mass-for-five-voices-i-kyrie/263774075?i=263774103" rel="noopener">Mass for five voices</a> | <a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/mass-for-four-voices-i-kyrie/263774075?i=263774697" rel="noopener">Mass for four voices</a> | <a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/mass-for-three-voices-i-kyrie/263774075?i=263775603" rel="noopener">Mass for three voices</a> | <a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/lord-in-they-wrath-correct-me-not-a3/1677637180?i=1677637289" rel="noopener">Lord in They Wrath Correct Me Not</a> |<a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/o-god-which-art-most-merciful-a3/1677637180?i=1677637290" rel="noopener">O God Which Art Most Merciful</a> | <a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/attend-mine-humble-prayer-lord-a3/1677637180?i=1677637299" rel="noopener">Attend Mine Humble Prayer Lord</a> | <a href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/ye-sacred-muses/1087244083?i=1087245029" rel="noopener">Ye Sacred Muses</a></p><p>Keyboard works:</p><p><a href="https://music.apple.com/us/playlist/william-byrd-pavan-and-galliard-mb29a/pl.u-KVXBD4VCZMAM407" rel="noopener">First Pavan &amp;
